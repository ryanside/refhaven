# Canvas

Each collection opens as its canvas: its images on a free canvas, with a layout, notes, and groups of its own. The library has no canvas. The user pans with the wheel, with space-drag, or with middle-drag, and zooms toward the cursor with Ctrl+wheel or a pinch, from 1% to 6400%. A click selects the topmost image under the cursor, shift-click adds or removes one, and a click on empty canvas clears the selection. A drag on empty canvas draws a marquee and selects every image it touches, and with Shift adds them. `Bring to front` and `Send to back`, or `]` and `[`, move the selection above or below every other image. Dragging a selected image moves the whole selection, and the move is saved when the drag ends. The selection has a frame with eight handles that resize it with a locked aspect ratio about the opposite point, and one above it that turns it about the frame's center, in steps of 15 degrees with Shift held. Another open page of the same user shows the move without a reload. Image files dropped on the canvas are saved and land where they were dropped, and a pasted or picked image lands at the center of the view. An image shows its 512 px thumbnail, and its 2048 px copy once it is large on screen. Delete, Backspace, or `Remove from collection` takes the selected images off the canvas and out of the collection. Undo and redo take back a move, a resize, a turn, a change of order, a removal, or an add for as long as the page is open. `Add note` or N opens a text field at the center of the view, and a double click on a note opens it. A note is plain text in one of six colors, chosen with `Note color`. It lives on its canvas only, and is selected, moved, resized, turned, restacked, removed, and undone like an image. `Group` or Mod+G puts the selected images and notes in a group, and `Ungroup` or Mod+Shift+G takes them out. A click, a shift-click, a marquee, or select all on any member of a group selects the whole group, so the group moves, resizes, turns, restacks, and leaves together.

## Sub-features

- `canvas-draw` draws each saved image at the position that `canvas.open` returns.
- `canvas-pan` moves the view by the scrolled amount, or with space-drag or middle-drag.
- `canvas-zoom` zooms toward the cursor with Ctrl+wheel or a pinch, from 1% to 6400%.
- `canvas-select` selects with click and shift-click, clears with a click on empty canvas, and outlines each selected image.
- `canvas-marquee` draws a rectangle from a press on empty canvas to the pointer, and on release selects every image whose own turned rectangle it touches. With Shift held it adds them to the selection. It saves nothing.
- `canvas-order` brings the selection to the front or sends it to the back, in its own order, with one `canvas.apply` call that patches only `z`, or several calls for more than 1,000 images, by `Bring to front`, `Send to back`, `]`, or `[`. The two keys also work with Option held, for keyboards that type a bracket that way. The drawn order, the click order, and `canvas.open` agree.
- `canvas-move` moves the selection with a drag and saves it as one `canvas.apply` call when the drag ends. A click saves nothing.
- `canvas-frame` draws the selection's frame: one image's own turned rectangle, or the upright box around several, with eight resize handles and one rotate handle that keep their size at every zoom.
- `canvas-resize` scales the selection by one factor about the frame's point opposite the dragged handle, for a corner and an edge alike, and stops when the frame's longer edge is 8 px on screen. The release saves one `canvas.apply` call that patches only `x`, `y`, and `scale`.
- `canvas-rotate` turns the selection about the frame's center by the angle the pointer swept. With Shift held, one image ends at a multiple of 15 degrees and several images turn by a multiple of 15 degrees. The release saves one `canvas.apply` call. One image turned about its own center is patched with `rotation` alone, and images that also moved with `rotation`, `x`, and `y`.
- `canvas-sync` shows a move made in another page of the same user, without a reload.
- `canvas-collection` shows a collection's images on its own canvas, the page `/c/{collectionId}` opens by default. A move there does not move the image on another collection's canvas.
- `canvas-drop` saves image files dropped on the canvas and places them with one `canvas.apply` add each: the first centered on the drop point, each next one 440 units to the right. The image joins the collection.
- `canvas-paste` places a pasted image, one picked with `Add images` in the panel, or a file dropped beside the canvas, at the center of the view, in a row the same way.
- `canvas-remove` removes the selected images with one `canvas.apply` call of `remove` ops, by Delete, Backspace, or `Remove from collection`, and the image leaves the collection.
- `canvas-undo` undoes and redoes the page's moves, resizes, turns, changes of order, removals, and adds with one `canvas.apply` call each, by Mod+Z, Mod+Shift+Z, Ctrl+Y, or the `Undo` and `Redo` buttons. An undo that the server state no longer allows shows "That change could not be undone." and changes nothing.
- `canvas-keys` maps Delete and Backspace, Mod+Z, Mod+Shift+Z, Ctrl+Y, Mod+A to select every item, Escape to clear the selection, `]` to bring the selection to the front, `[` to send it to the back, N to add a note, Mod+G to group the selection, and Mod+Shift+G to ungroup it. No shortcut fires while a text field has focus.
- `canvas-note` adds a note with `Add note` or N, edits one on a double click in a textarea over it, saves on leaving the field or Mod+Enter with one `canvas.apply` call, discards on Escape, removes a note saved with no text, and recolors the selected notes with `Note color`. Each is one undo entry. A note is not in a gallery or in search.
- `canvas-group` groups a selection of two or more items with `Group` or Mod+G in one `canvas.apply` call of `addGroup` and one `parent` patch per item, or in several calls from 1,000 items up, and ungroups each group of the selection with `Ungroup` or Mod+Shift+G with one `remove` op per group, in one call up to 1,000 groups. A removed group's members keep their `parent`, which counts only while its group is on the canvas, so undo brings a group back with one `addGroup` and no patch. Selecting any member selects every member of its group, and a shift-click on a selected member takes its whole group out of the selection. `Group` is disabled with fewer than two items selected or with one whole group selected, and `Ungroup` with no group in the selection. Each is one undo entry. A group has no outline of its own, only the selection's frame.
- `canvas-display` swaps an image's thumbnail for its 2048 px copy once its longer edge passes 512 device pixels on screen, at the same size.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Choose a collection's link in the sidebar. `/c/{collectionId}` with no `view` is its canvas.
- Choose `Canvas` on a collection's gallery, or open `/c/{collectionId}?view=canvas` directly.
- Choose `Gallery` on the canvas to see the same collection as a gallery, at `/c/{collectionId}?view=gallery`.
- `/library` is a gallery only, with no `Canvas` link. `/library?view=canvas` shows the gallery too, and keeps `view` in the URL.

## Driving it with Playwright

Preconditions:

- `verify.sh doctor` reports that the deployment has the four R2 variables.
- A new user, signed in with `signInWithPasskey`, with a collection that holds images. Make it with `collectionCanvas(page)` from `apps/web/checks/canvas-screen.ts`, which opens its gallery, and save the images there through the Library recipe, or add saved ones with `collections.addRefs`. Fill each image with one color, so its pixels on screen are known. Save one image over 2048 px to get a 2048 px copy.
- A known viewport, for example `await page.setViewportSize({ width: 1400, height: 900 })`.

- **Open.** Run `await page.goto("http://localhost:3000/c/<collectionId>")`. Wait for `[data-testid="canvas"][data-shown-thumbnails="N"]`, where N is the number of the collection's images that fit in the view.
- **Readout.** `page.getByTestId("canvas-readout")` carries `data-items`, `data-zoom` in percent, `data-camera-x` and `data-camera-y`, and `data-selected`. The camera is the world point at the canvas's top-left corner. An item at world point `p` is at `(p - camera) * zoom / 100` from the top-left of `page.getByTestId("canvas")`.
- **Draw.** Take a screenshot and read it with `PNG.sync.read` from `pngjs`. The image's color fills the screen rectangle that follows from `canvas.open` through the API (`clientFor(page)`) and the readout.
- **Pan.** Run `await page.mouse.move(x, y)` and `await page.mouse.wheel(80, 150)`. The camera moves by `80 / zoom` and `150 / zoom` world units.
- **Zoom.** Hold `Control` with `page.keyboard.down("Control")` and run `page.mouse.wheel(0, -100)`. The world point under the cursor is the same before and after. Many large steps stop at `data-zoom="6400"` and `data-zoom="1"`.
- **Select.** Run `page.mouse.click(x, y)` on an image. `data-selected` becomes 1, and `[data-outline-key="<item key>"]` covers the image's screen rectangle. Hold `Shift` and click a second image for 2. Click empty canvas for 0.
- **Marquee.** Run `page.mouse.move` to empty canvas, `page.mouse.down()`, `page.mouse.move(x, y, { steps: 10 })`, and `page.mouse.up()`. During the drag `[data-marquee]` covers the rectangle from the press to the pointer, and after the release it is gone. `data-selected` counts the images the rectangle touched, and `[data-outline-key]` names them. Hold `Shift` before the press to add to the selection. No `canvas:apply` frame is sent.
- **Order.** Select images and click `page.getByRole("button", { name: "Bring to front" })` or `"Send to back"`, or press `]` or `[`. One `canvas:apply` frame patches only `z` of each selected image. `canvas.open` returns the new z values, and the pixels where two images overlap show the one on top. Both buttons are disabled with nothing selected.
- **Frame.** With one image selected, `[data-frame] [data-handle]` counts 9. The handles are named `nw`, `n`, `ne`, `e`, `se`, `s`, `sw`, `w`, and `rotate`. Read a handle's center from its `boundingBox()`. The rotate handle is 24 px above the middle of the frame's top edge, turned with the frame. With nothing selected there is no `[data-frame]`.
- **Resize.** Round a handle's center, then `page.mouse.move` there, `page.mouse.down()`, `page.mouse.move(x, y, { steps: 10 })`, and `page.mouse.up()`. `canvas.open` returns the new `scale`, `x`, and `y`, and the point of the frame opposite the handle is where it was. The factor is 1 plus the pointer's travel along the line from that point to the handle, over that line's length squared.
- **Rotate.** Drag the `rotate` handle around the frame's center. `rotation` grows by the swept angle. Hold `Shift` with `page.keyboard.down("Shift")` before the drag to snap. A press on any handle changes nothing until the pointer has moved 3 pixels.
- **Apply calls.** Listen with `page.on("websocket")` and `framesent` before the page loads. Each save is one frame with `type` `Mutation` and `udfPath` `canvas:apply`, and its `args[0].ops` show the fields each patch sets.
- **Move.** Run `page.mouse.down()`, `page.mouse.move(x, y, { steps: 8 })`, and `page.mouse.up()` on a selected image. `canvas.changes` from the earlier `rev` returns only the moved items, at their old position plus the screen distance divided by the zoom, and `rev` rose by one.
- **Sync.** Open a second page in the same browser context with `page.context().newPage()`. It shares the session. A move in the first page shows in the second one's pixels without a reload.
- **Display copy.** Hold requests to paths ending in `/2048.webp` with `page.route`. Zoom in until an image's longer edge passes 512 px on screen. The page asks for its 2048 px copy. Release the requests, then wait for `[data-testid="canvas"][data-display-keys~="<item key>"]`. The image covers the same screen rectangle before and after.
- **Collection.** Open `/c/{collectionId}`. The readout's `data-items` is the number of the collection's images, and only their colors are drawn. Read its layout with `canvas.open` and `{ collectionId }` through the API. A drag there changes that canvas's `rev` and not another collection's.
- **Drop.** Send a `drop` that carries files to `[data-testid="canvas"]` with `send` from `apps/web/checks/browser.ts`, with the drop point as its last argument. One `[role="status"] li` per file appears in the panel. Wait for `data-shown-thumbnails` to count the new images. `canvas.open` returns the first centered on `toWorld` of the drop point and each next one `placedEdge + placementGap` to the right.
- **Paste or pick.** Send a `paste` that carries a file, or run `page.getByLabel("Add images").setInputFiles(...)` on the canvas page. The image lands centered on the world point at the center of `[data-testid="canvas"]`.
- **Add through the API.** `canvas.apply` with `{ op: "add", refId, x, y }` places a Ref at a point, brings back a removed item, or moves a live one, and makes the Ref a member of the collection.
- **Remove.** Select an image and run `page.keyboard.press("Delete")`. `data-items` drops by one, `canvas.open` no longer returns the image, and the canvas's `rev` rose by one. `page.getByRole("button", { name: "Remove from collection" })` does the same, and is disabled with nothing selected. The image leaves the collection's gallery and stays on `/library`.
- **Undo and redo.** Run `page.keyboard.press("ControlOrMeta+z")`, `"ControlOrMeta+Shift+z"`, or `"Control+y"`, or click `Undo` or `Redo`. Each is one `canvas.apply` call. A removal comes back with the same `x`, `y`, `scale`, and `z`. `Redo` is disabled after a new edit.
- **Select all.** Run `page.keyboard.press("ControlOrMeta+a")`. `data-selected` equals `data-items`. `Escape` sets it to 0.
- **Add a note.** Click `page.getByRole("button", { name: "Add note" })` or press `n`. `page.getByLabel("Note", { exact: true })` is a focused textarea centered on `[data-testid="canvas"]`. Type with `page.keyboard.type`, then press `ControlOrMeta+Enter`, or click empty canvas. `data-items` rises by one, and one `canvas:apply` frame carries one `addNote` op. `canvas.open` returns the note with `kind` `note`, its `text`, `color`, `width`, and `height`, and its pixels in its color inside the rectangle that `width * scale` and `height * scale` give.
- **Edit a note.** Run `page.mouse.dblclick(x, y)` on a note. The textarea holds its text. Leaving it saves one patch of `text`, `width`, `height`, and, when the box changed, `x` and `y`, so its top-left corner stays. `Escape` saves nothing. A note left with no text is removed.
- **Note color.** With notes selected, run `page.getByLabel("Note color").selectOption("blue")`. One patch of `color` per selected note. With nothing selected it sets the color of the next new note.
- **Group.** Select two items, then press `ControlOrMeta+g` or click `page.getByRole("button", { name: "Group", exact: true })`. One `canvas:apply` frame carries one `addGroup` op and one patch of `parent` per item. `canvas.open` through the API returns an item with `kind` `group` and only a `key`, and each member with that key as its `parent`. Click empty canvas, then one member: `data-selected` counts every member, `[data-outline-key]` names each, and one `[data-frame]` covers them all.
- **Ungroup.** With a member selected, press `ControlOrMeta+Shift+g` or click `Ungroup`. One `canvas:apply` frame carries one `remove` op per group. `canvas.open` no longer returns the group. Each member still has its `parent`, which now names no group on the canvas, and a click selects one item again.
- **Proof.** Screenshot the canvas after each step that changes it. Read the saved positions with `canvas.open` or `canvas.changes` through the API, or with `verify.sh rows canvasItems`, filtered to the canvas's ID.

`apps/web/checks/canvas-page.ts` runs this journey. `apps/web/checks/canvas-order.ts` covers the marquee, a turned image, bring to front and send to back, their undo, the keys, and a second page. `apps/web/checks/canvas-transform.ts` covers the frame, resizing, rotating, and their undo. `apps/web/checks/canvas-undo.ts` covers removing, undo and redo, and the keymap. `apps/web/checks/canvas-drop.ts` covers dropping, pasting, and picking images on the canvas. `apps/web/checks/canvas-notes-page.ts` covers notes on the page, and `apps/web/checks/canvas-notes.ts` the note ops of `canvas.apply`. `apps/web/checks/canvas-groups-page.ts` covers groups on the page, and `apps/web/checks/canvas-groups.ts` the group ops of `canvas.apply`. `apps/web/checks/canvas.ts` covers the canvas functions, the refused library canvas, and the rules between two users, and `apps/web/checks/canvas-add.ts` covers the `add` op. `apps/web/checks/collection-canvas.ts` covers membership and a collection's canvas through the API. `apps/web/checks/renderer.ts` draws a local bitmap with the renderer and needs neither sign-in nor R2.

## Gotchas

- Chromium sends mouse events at whole pixels. Round a computed point before you move the mouse to it, or the world point under the cursor drifts by a fraction of a pixel.
- The readout changes before the next frame draws. Wait two animation frames before a screenshot or an outline count.
- `/c/{collectionId}` opens the canvas, which has no cards. A collection's gallery, with its cards and its rename form, is at `?view=gallery`.
- The panel with the readout is a column 320 px wide, left of the canvas, with its own scroll. It covers no part of the canvas. The view links are in the bar above the canvas.
- The sidebar takes 256 px of the page's width and the canvas's panel 320 px. `canvasViewport` in `apps/web/checks/canvas-screen.ts` is a page of 1976 by 900, which leaves the canvas 1400 wide. A narrower page cuts off items that a check places near the right edge.
- `canvas.open` through the API is a mutation, and it places any image of the collection that has no item yet. Put every image in the collection before you open the canvas, or the page and the API see different items. An image saved on `/library` is in no collection.
- An image that has no 2048 px copy, because it is 2048 px or smaller, loads its original for the display role. Only an image over 2048 px requests `2048.webp`.
- `canvas.open` and `canvas.changes` return group items, which have no place. `openCanvas` and `canvasChanges` in `apps/web/checks/canvas-screen.ts` return the placed items only.
- An item without a loaded texture is not drawn. With more images in view than the thumbnail budget of 200, the rest stay empty.
- `page.waitForURL` resolves before the new page renders. Wait for something on the new page, such as the collection's heading, before you use a control that both pages have, like `Add images`.
- The canvas fits the camera to every item once, when the page opens, at no more than 100%. Reload to reset the camera.
- The panel holds `Add images`, the outcome list, and the buttons. It grows downward and scrolls, so the canvas keeps its place and size when an outcome or a notice appears.
- `data-shown-thumbnails="0"` shows before the first snapshot loads. An image is placed when its save ends, so a drop that early is still placed once the snapshot has loaded by then.
- A selected image's outline is 2 px wide and covers its edges. Allow 3 px when you compare its drawn rectangle.
- The note editor is the canvas page's one text field. Open it with `Add note` or a double click on a note, and find it with `page.getByLabel("Note")`. While it has focus no canvas shortcut fires: `n`, Backspace, and Mod+A edit the text. It stops at 2,000 characters.
- Give a second page the same viewport as the first, or its view is fitted differently and a point you read is somewhere else.
- A press near a selected image's corner, edge middle, or above its top edge lands on a handle and resizes or turns instead of selecting. Click other images away from those points.
- A handle of a turned frame is a turned square, so its bounding box is larger than 10 px. Compare sizes between zooms at one rotation.
- A drag that ends where it began saves nothing, and so does a press on a handle that does not move.
- A marquee must start on empty canvas. A press on an image starts a drag, and a press on a handle a resize or a turn.
- An image lies on top of another by its `z`, not by the order it was saved in. Set `z` through the API with a patch to know which image is on top.
- A selected note's outline and the frame are drawn in a near-black color on the note's edges. Read a note's text pixels a few pixels inside its rectangle.
- A page is light unless a theme is chosen, because headless Chromium's system is light. Every color here is for the light page. On the dark page the outline and the frame are near white, and a `black` note is drawn near white, `#fafaf9`.
- A note's text has antialiased edges. Count the pixels near its color inside its rectangle, rather than reading one point.
- The readout counts items, notes included, and `data-shown-thumbnails` counts images only.
- A new note's scale is one over the zoom when it was added, so its type shows at 16 px.
